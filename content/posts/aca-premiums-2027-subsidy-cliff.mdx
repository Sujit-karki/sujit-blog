## What's happening for 2027

Per KFF's July 2026 analysis of preliminary rate filings across 16 states and DC, ACA Marketplace insurers are proposing a **median premium increase of 14% for 2027** — a likely second consecutive year of double-digit increases. Of the 77 insurers that had filed by mid-July, most are requesting increases between 10% and 20%, and 20 insurers are requesting more than 20%. If these hold through the rate-review process (rates are finalized in late summer), typical premiums for participating insurers would climb by more than a third between 2025 and 2027.

## The subsidy cliff, explained

Here's the mechanic that traps people. With the enhanced credits gone, the old
**400%-of-federal-poverty-level cliff is back**. Below 400% FPL you get a tax
credit on a sliding scale. At 400% or above you can lose *all* premium
assistance and pay the full, unsubsidised rate.

So the number that actually matters is the dollar income where that happens —
and it depends entirely on how many people are in your household. Marketplace
eligibility for a coverage year uses the poverty guidelines published the
*previous* calendar year, so **2027 coverage is governed by the 2026 guidelines**
the Department of Health and Human Services published in January.

Each additional person adds **$5,680** to the guideline, so **$22,720** to the
cliff. Alaska and Hawaii have their own, higher guidelines — for a single person
those run $19,950 and $18,360 respectively, pushing the cliff to $79,800 and
$73,440.

<InfoBox type="warning">
This is a true cliff, not a ramp. A single dollar of extra income across that
line can cost thousands in lost subsidy — the classic case is a household of one
at $63,839 versus $63,841. If you are anywhere near the threshold, reducing MAGI
through HSA or pre-tax retirement contributions can be worth considerably more
than the raise that pushed you over it.
</InfoBox>

<InfoBox type="tip">
**Correction, 7 September 2026.** This post originally gave the single-person
cliff as "roughly $62,600". The correct figure from the 2026 HHS guidelines is
**$63,840**. The error mattered in the wrong direction — it would have told
someone earning $63,000 that they had lost all subsidy when they had not.
</InfoBox>

There is a second trap underneath the first one, and it catches people who never
thought the cliff applied to them. The premium tax credit is paid in advance,
month by month, based on the income you *estimate* when you enrol. It is then
reconciled against your actual income when you file. If you estimated below 400%
and finished the year above it, you do not simply lose next year's help — you
repay the whole advance credit you already received, as a lump sum, at tax time.

That is why this matters to anyone with variable income: freelancers, people on
commission, anyone who might take on a late-year contract or realise a capital
gain. A profitable December can retroactively convert a year of subsidised
premiums into a bill. The people most exposed are precisely those who cannot
predict their income in November, when they have to state it.

The practical consequence is that if your income is uncertain and might land near
the threshold, estimating it *high* is often the safer error. Under-claiming
means a refund when you reconcile. Over-claiming means writing a cheque.

## Who gets hit hardest

The 2027 increases primarily hit enrollees **just above 400% FPL** who no longer qualify for any subsidy at all. People below that level still get some tax credit, but with benchmark premiums themselves rising, the dollar amount they owe out of pocket climbs too — and the subsidy cliff's return is expected to push more people out of the marketplace entirely.

## Here's What I'd Actually Do

1. **Map your MAGI against 400% FPL before December.** If you're close, model whether an HSA or pre-tax retirement contribution keeps you under the cliff.
2. **Shop, don't auto-renew.** With this much repricing, last year's best plan may be badly overpriced now. Compare at healthcare.gov during open enrollment.
3. **Weigh premium vs. deductible.** Many insurers are pricing plans with higher deductibles for lower premiums — run your expected usage before picking a plan on premium alone.
4. **Mark the calendar: Nov 1, 2026 – Jan 15, 2027.** Miss it and you're locked out absent a qualifying life event.
