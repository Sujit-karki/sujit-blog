## The appeal of the holiday flip

Holiday season creates a predictable pattern: doorbuster deals, clearance markdowns, and a wave of gift returns that all funnel into the secondhand market. Flippers who buy low during sales and resell into post-holiday demand can genuinely make money — the opportunity is real. The problem is how the profit gets calculated in most "look what I made" posts.

The "sticker profit" on this example — sale price minus what you paid — looks like $60. The real take-home, after everything else, is closer to a third of the sale price. That gap is exactly what trips up new flippers who price based on the first number instead of the last one.

## The fee stack nobody adds up

Marketplace fees generally apply to the **total** transaction — item price plus shipping plus tax collected — not just the item price alone, and there's often a small per-order fee layered on top of the percentage. Fee schedules vary by category and by platform, and they change periodically, so the exact number matters less than the habit of checking it before you price a listing.

## The tax nobody budgets for

If reselling is an ongoing activity for you — not just occasionally clearing out your own closet — the IRS generally treats your net profit as **self-employment income**, subject to the **15.3% self-employment tax** on top of regular income tax. That's Social Security and Medicare tax that an employer would normally split with you; as a self-employed seller, you pay both halves.

Separately, the federal **1099-K reporting threshold** is back to **$20,000 in gross payments and 200 transactions** for a marketplace to be required to send you the form. That threshold only controls whether you *receive a form* — it has never controlled whether the income is *taxable*. Income below the reporting threshold is still income.

## Here's What I'd Actually Do

1. **Price backward from your target take-home, not forward from the sticker cost.** Decide the margin you actually want after fees, shipping, and tax, then set your sale price to hit it.
2. **Set aside roughly 15–20% of net profit for taxes as you go**, especially once you're clearly running this as an ongoing activity rather than occasional selling.
3. **Track cost basis for every item**, including what you paid and any costs to prep or ship it — you'll need it whether or not a 1099-K arrives.
4. **Check the actual fee schedule on your platform before listing**, not from memory or an old screenshot — fees and category rules shift.
5. **Treat reselling profit like any other income when it comes to saving**, folding it into a real budget rather than treating it as free money — my [50/30/20 budget guide](/posts/budgeting-50-30-20-rule) works as well for side income as for a paycheck.
