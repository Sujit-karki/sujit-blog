Every fall, a chunk of families leave real money on the table simply by filing the FAFSA late — not because they weren't eligible, but because some aid runs out before they applied. With the 2027-28 form on track to open October 1, here's what to actually do about it.

## Why October 1 matters

Congress requires the FAFSA to open no later than October 1 each year, and the Department of Education has said it's on track to hit that date, or earlier, for the 2027-28 cycle. That's a meaningful contrast to the rocky rollout years the form had earlier in the decade, when delays pushed the opening well past October and created chaos for families and college financial aid offices alike.

## Prior-prior-year income, explained

The FAFSA uses what's called "prior-prior-year" income — for the 2027-28 award year, that means your **2025** tax return, which you've almost certainly already filed. This system exists specifically so families don't have to rush a fresh tax return just to file the FAFSA on time. If your 2025 return is done, you already have everything the form needs on the income side.

<InfoBox type="info">
If your financial situation has changed significantly since 2025 — job loss, a pay cut, a new child — most schools have a process called "professional judgment" or a special circumstances appeal where a financial aid office can adjust your aid package based on your current reality, not just the tax year the form uses.
</InfoBox>

## First-come, first-served aid

Here's the distinction that trips people up: the FAFSA's federal deadline is far out, but a lot of the money that actually matters isn't federal, and isn't governed by that deadline at all. Many state grant programs and college-specific aid funds are awarded first-come, first-served, and can be fully allocated weeks or months before the federal cutoff.

## What to have ready before you file

You'll move fastest if you have: your 2025 tax return (or your parents', if you're a dependent student), records of untaxed income if applicable, current bank and investment balances, and your Federal Student Aid (FSA) ID set up in advance — that last step alone can add days if you wait until the form opens to create it.

## Here's What I'd Actually Do

1. **Set up your FSA ID now**, before the form even opens — it's a five-minute task that otherwise becomes a bottleneck on day one.
2. **File in the first week or two after it opens**, especially if you're a first-time filer at a school with limited institutional aid.
3. **Check your state's specific deadlines separately.** Many states publish their own FAFSA priority deadlines that are earlier and stricter than the federal one.
4. **Renewing filers shouldn't skip the urgency either** — returning students competing for the same limited pools benefit from the same early-filing logic.
5. **If your finances changed since 2025, contact the financial aid office directly** rather than assuming the FAFSA's income figure is the final word.
