## What actually happened

Self-reported income surveys have always been the weak link in understanding gig pay — drivers round up, remember good weeks better than bad ones, and rarely subtract their actual costs. Gridwise's 2026 report changed the data quality bar by GPS-tracking 101,709 real drivers' trips directly, rather than asking them to estimate. The headline finding: median trip pay came in at $14.07 per hour, rising to $15.03 with bonuses and incentives included. That's meaningfully below the "$20-something an hour" figure that circulates in a lot of gig-work recruiting material.

The distribution matters as much as the median. The top 25% of tracked drivers earned $17.02 an hour or more; the top 10% cleared $20.83 or more. That spread reflects real, controllable factors — which markets, which time blocks, which platforms, and how selectively a driver accepts offers — not just luck. Median pay is a starting point for expectations, not a ceiling or a guarantee for any individual driver's actual results.

## The math for one person

Gross per-trip pay is only step one. Two deductions specific to gig work turn that number into something meaningfully smaller — and neither shows up anywhere in the app's earnings screen.

The first is the mileage deduction, and it's a genuine tax benefit gig drivers should be using aggressively, not a cost. The IRS lets self-employed drivers deduct a standard rate per business mile — 72.5 cents through June 30, 2026, then 76 cents from July 1 onward, a mid-year bump that's easy to miss if you're not tracking dates carefully. That deduction reduces your taxable income, not your gross pay directly, but the tax savings it generates functionally raises your real hourly take-home once you account for it properly.

The second is self-employment tax: 15.3% on your net self-employment earnings, covering both the employee and employer shares of Social Security and Medicare. A W-2 employee only feels half that burden directly, with their employer quietly covering the other half. A gig driver, classified as an independent contractor, owes the whole thing themselves, on top of regular federal and state income tax.

<InfoBox type="tip">
Log every mile from the moment you go online to the moment you go offline, not just miles with a passenger or order in the car — "deadhead" miles driving to a pickup or repositioning between orders are generally deductible business miles too, and skipping them meaningfully understates your real deduction.
</InfoBox>

Put both pieces together and a driver earning $15/hr gross, driving roughly 12 miles per hour of active driving, is sitting on a real mileage deduction worth $9.12/hr at the new 76-cent rate — a substantial reduction to taxable income before the 15.3% self-employment tax and regular income tax get applied to whatever's left.

## The case for doing it anyway

None of this is an argument that gig delivery is a bad way to earn money — it's an argument for knowing your real number before you commit meaningful hours to it. For the right person, the flexibility genuinely matters more than squeezing out the last dollar of hourly pay: no schedule commitment, no interview, income available same-day in a genuine emergency.

## Here's What I'd Actually Do

1. **Track your own actual hourly pay for two weeks before assuming the app's advertised numbers apply to you** — your specific market and hours matter more than any national average.
2. **Log every business mile, including deadhead miles**, using a dedicated mileage-tracking app rather than trying to reconstruct it later from memory.
3. **Set aside roughly 25–30% of net gig income for taxes** as a starting estimate, adjusting based on your actual bracket and state — self-employment tax catches new drivers off guard every filing season.
4. **Run multiple apps at once if your market supports it** — it's the most consistently cited lever for pushing toward top-quartile pay.
5. **Compare your real net hourly, not gross, against actual alternatives** before deciding gig work is or isn't worth your time relative to other options available to you.
6. **Make quarterly estimated tax payments if this becomes a meaningful income source**, to avoid an underpayment penalty at filing time.
