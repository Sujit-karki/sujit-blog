If you're on Medicare or getting close to it, two numbers move every year in opposite directions on your monthly check: your Social Security cost-of-living adjustment, and your Part B premium. Here's what's projected for 2027 and how to think about the interaction before open enrollment.

## The projection

The Medicare Trustees' 2026 report projects the standard Part B premium at roughly **$209.50 a month for 2027**, up from the confirmed **$202.90** in 2026 — a smaller percentage jump than 2026's own increase over the prior year. The Trustees also project the Part D base premium around **$41.33**.

## It's only a projection until November

Every year, the Trustees Report gives an early estimate for the following year's Part B premium, and every year the actual number — set by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services — can land higher or lower once final program spending data is in. Treat the $209.50 figure as a well-informed estimate, not a locked number, until CMS makes its official announcement, typically in the fall.

<InfoBox type="info">
Last year's early Trustees projection for the following year came in higher than what CMS eventually confirmed. Projections can move in either direction — don't lock your budget around the estimate alone.
</InfoBox>

## The COLA offset problem

Here's the mechanic that trips people up every year. Your Social Security cost-of-living adjustment is a **percentage** of your current benefit. Your Part B premium increase is a **flat dollar amount**, usually deducted directly from that same benefit check. Those two numbers don't automatically net out in your favor — the smaller your benefit, the more a flat premium increase eats into a percentage-based raise.

## IRMAA and open enrollment

Higher-income beneficiaries pay more than the standard Part B premium through IRMAA, the income-related monthly adjustment amount, based on your income from **two years prior** — so your 2027 IRMAA bracket is determined by your **2025** income. If you've had a life-changing event since then — retirement, divorce, the death of a spouse — you can request a reconsideration rather than accept a bracket based on outdated income.

Separately, Medicare's annual **open enrollment window runs October 15 through December 7**, during which you can switch between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage, or change your Part D drug plan. This window is unrelated to when the Part B premium itself is announced, but the two tend to get discussed together every fall.

## Here's What I'd Actually Do

1. **Don't finalize your 2027 budget around the Trustees Report number alone.** Wait for CMS's official confirmation before treating it as fixed.
2. **Check your IRMAA exposure now if your income is near a bracket threshold.** A modest change in reported income can push you into a higher surcharge tier.
3. **Use the open enrollment window deliberately.** Compare your current Medicare Advantage or Part D plan against alternatives every year — formularies and provider networks change even when you don't.
4. **If your income dropped since 2025, file for an IRMAA reconsideration** rather than assuming you're stuck with a bracket based on higher, outdated income.
5. **Think of the COLA and Part B premium as one combined number**, not two separate pieces of good and bad news — your real raise is the net of both.
